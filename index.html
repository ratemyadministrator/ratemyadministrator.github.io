How it works

Two-minute walkthrough: posting a review step by step, then six ways to protect yourself. No sound; all instructions are on screen and written out below.

  1. Find your institution

    Search 3,239 US community colleges, state universities, and private nonprofit colleges.

  2. Find or add the administrator

    Search people already listed there. If they're missing, add their name and title. Matching names at the same college are merged, so each person is listed once.

  3. Rate and write

    Ten 1-to-5 ratings, optional yes/no questions about your experience, a renewal vote, and a written review.

  4. Post anonymously

    No email, no name, no verification. The review shows only what you choose: your status, role, years, and the last year you worked under this person.

Protecting yourself

  • Describe patterns, not incidents. "Budget cuts are announced without asking the departments affected" could come from many people. "At the Oct. 4 meeting he told me my lab would close" points to a few.
  • Leave out details only you would know: dates of specific meetings or emails, a policy number cited against you, a program only you teach, your exact department or title.
  • Post from your own phone or computer, on cellular data or home internet, not a work computer or campus Wi-Fi. A VPN adds another layer.
  • Timing can point to you. Wait a while after a specific event, especially a small meeting, before posting.
  • What the site keeps: your ratings, answers, written review, and the status, role, and years you choose to show. Never stored with a review: your name, email, account, or IP address. Yes/no answers appear only after at least 3 people answer.
  • Keep it about conduct in the role. No student names, no health, family, or personal-life details, nothing you can't support. Reviews that read as personal attacks are removed.
  • Editing your review. This browser remembers your reviews, so reviewing the same person again replaces your earlier review. Another device posts a new one.

Limits to know

  • Nothing confirms reviewers work there. Anyone can post, including about colleges they never worked at. Read scores alongside the number of reviews and the written detail.
  • No accounts, no IP addresses saved. The site stores only what you type and select. Like any web service, the database host keeps short-lived request logs that can include IP addresses; a VPN adds a layer of protection.
  • Moderation. Readers can report a review; three reports hide it until the site owner checks it. The owner can also hide any review or administrator listing.
